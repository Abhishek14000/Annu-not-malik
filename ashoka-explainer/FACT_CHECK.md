# Fact check: "The King Who Apologized in Stone"

Sources: Ashoka's edicts in the standard translations (Hultzsch, *Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum* I, 1925; S. Dhammika, *The Edicts of King Asoka*, 1993), Romila Thapar, *Aśoka and the Decline of the Mauryas*, and William Reddy, *The Navigation of Feeling* (2001).

## The four checks you asked for

| Claim | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| The numbers and the remorse come from Rock Edict XIII, and 261 BCE is a conventional date | ✅ Confirmed | RE XIII gives 150,000 deported, 100,000 killed, and "many times that number" who died. It places the war 8 years after Ashoka's consecration, which is usually dated c. 261/260 BCE. "c." on screen is right. The script now adds "and many more dead", which is in the edict. |
| Daily self-examination for anger, cruelty, pride and envy is Pillar Edict III | ⚠️ Partly right, so fixed | PE III does list *fierceness, cruelty, anger, pride and envy* as the things that lead to sin, and tells the reader to watch for them. It is addressed to **people in general, not officials**, and it **does not say "daily"**. New line: "he urges people to watch themselves for cruelty, anger, pride and envy." PE III is on the Delhi-Topra and Lauriya Nandangarh pillars, so those visuals still fit. (An instruction to *officials* to avoid envy, anger and cruelty does exist, in Separate Kalinga Edict I at Dhauli and Jaugada. You could use that instead, but then cite it rather than PE III.) |
| The warning to forest tribes is also in Rock Edict XIII | ✅ Confirmed | RE XIII tells the forest peoples that, even though he repents, he has the power to punish them, so they should be ashamed of their wrongs and not be killed. |
| "Emotives" is William Reddy's term, so cut the 0:58 beat | ✅ Done | The term comes from Reddy's *The Navigation of Feeling* (2001). The beat is cut, so no quote card or photo is needed. |

## Other changes

1. **Dhauli does not carry Rock Edict XIII.** At the two edict sites in Kalinga (Dhauli and Jaugada), Rock Edicts XI–XIII are left out and two "Separate Edicts" appear in their place. The draft put "Rock Edict XIII" over the Dhauli site, which would be wrong on screen. The label now goes over Kalsi or Shahbazgarhi, where RE XIII does appear, and Dhauli is shown as "Kalinga today". The omission also directly supports the "feeling or performance?" question, so the turn now includes one line about it: "And in Kalinga itself, the confession is left out." Scholars disagree about why it was left out (tact or politics), and the line doesn't take a side. If you want to stay strictly with the draft, cut that line and the "Left out in Kalinga" card. That saves about 4 seconds.
2. **"One last holdout" became "an independent kingdom."** Kalinga was not the last unconquered region. The Tamil kingdoms of the far south (Cholas, Pandyas, Keralaputras, Satiyaputras) stayed outside the empire, and the edicts themselves name them as neighbours. The map should not show the whole subcontinent filled in.
3. **"Placed where thousands would read them" became "meant to be read aloud to crowds."** Few people could read, and the edicts themselves tell officials to read them aloud at set times, for example on Tishya days in the Separate Edicts.
4. **"He keeps his army" became "he keeps the death penalty."** No edict says the army was kept. Historians infer that, and it's true, but the edicts never state it. Pillar Edict IV keeps capital punishment and adds a three-day reprieve for condemned prisoners. That point comes straight from the edicts, so it's easier to defend.
5. **Medicine for people and animals, and wells along roads:** ✅ Rock Edict II, repeated in Pillar Edict VII (wells, shade trees and rest houses along roads). No change.
6. **"The first time a ruler let us read his emotions" became "a rare moment where a ruler lets us read his emotions."** "First" can't be defended. Earlier royal inscriptions and letters (Egyptian, Mesopotamian, Achaemenid) express royal feeling. What is genuinely rare is a ruler publicly regretting his own conquest.
7. **"Apology" became "regret" / "He confessed."** In RE XIII Ashoka expresses remorse and sorrow, but he doesn't apologise *to* the Kalingans. The title "The King Who Apologized in Stone" is acceptable as a hook. The narration and the text card now say exactly what the edict says.
8. **"Largest empire India had yet seen":** ✅ This is the standard view and is fine.
